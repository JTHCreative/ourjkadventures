# Pricing & Loyalty

*A companion to the Manifesto. An internal document. On what we charge, and why.*

---

**We do not put pins on sale.**

No sitewide discounts. No seasonal clearance. No "20% off this weekend only." No discount codes floating around in some influencer's bio. Not even a quiet markdown on last season's run once it stops moving.

This isn't a pricing tactic. It follows directly from what we are.

---

## Why a sale is the wrong move for us

A sale is a message, whether we mean it to be or not. It tells the person standing in front of the pin: *what we charged you a month ago wasn't really what this is worth.* For a lot of businesses that message costs nothing, because the product was never about meaning to begin with.

Ours is different. We already said it in the Manifesto — a pin isn't decoration, it's a flag for something someone loves. You don't discount a flag. Nobody marks down the price of a thing that says *this is one of mine.* The moment we do, we've told the customer the object was never that important to us either, and if it wasn't important to us, why would it be important to them?

There's a second, quieter reason. A sale trains people to wait. It teaches your best, most enthusiastic customers — the ones who'd buy the trail pin the day it drops because they *hiked that exact trail* — that patience gets rewarded better than love does. That's exactly backwards for a business built on people acting on enthusiasm in the moment.

---

## What we do instead: reward, not discount

The distinction that matters isn't "cheaper vs. full price." It's *what the money-off is for.*

A discount rewards **hesitation** — you get the deal because you waited, shopped around, or showed up during a promo window. It has nothing to do with who you are.

A loyalty reward does the opposite. It rewards **belonging** — you get it because you're already one of ours. You bought the first drop. You've got four pins on your board already. You referred a friend who also turned out to love this stuff. The reward is a thank-you for being in the room, not a discount for almost not showing up.

That's a distinction we protect, not blur. A loyalty reward should never be reachable by a stranger who just found the site — it should require having actually been a customer first.

The kinds of things that fit this: points toward a free pin after a certain number of purchases, early access to a new drop before it goes public, a small gift added to an order once someone crosses a collection milestone, a referral perk that benefits both people, a birthday or "collection anniversary" nod. What they have in common: none of them lower the price of the object. They add something *on top of* a relationship, rather than taking something *off* a price tag.

---

## The Collector's Path

This is where the philosophy becomes a real mechanism.

**Every full-price pin purchased from the core catalog earns a charge. Charges unlock rewards at 3, 6, and 10 — the tenth being a free pin. Redeeming the tenth resets the count to zero. There is no expiration.**

A few deliberate choices sit inside that structure, and each one exists to keep this from quietly turning into the sale we said we'd never run:

- **Every reward on the path is something added, never something subtracted.** No tier is a percentage or dollar amount off a future purchase — that would just rebuild a sale with extra steps. The shape holds at every stage: 3 charges unlocks early access to new drops before they go public; 6 charges adds a small physical piece that makes an existing collection better — an upgraded backing card, a mini sticker sheet — not a discount on the next one; 10 charges is a pin, free. The price of any single pin, on any single day, never moves.
- **It's all-or-nothing at the top tier, not partial.** There's no version of this where a card sits at 70% and gets applied to knock a few dollars off a purchase. Nine charges in, pin ten is still full price. The tenth charge only ever produces one outcome: a pin that's completely free.
- **Ten is a milestone, not a threshold.** High enough to mean something — this is for someone who has actually built a collection with us — while reachable within how people realistically buy over time. Three and six exist so the relationship has something to notice earlier than that.
- **Nothing expires.** A collector building slowly and a collector building fast get treated identically. Nothing here is designed to create urgency or a "don't lose your progress" pressure — that belongs to a different kind of business than this one.
- **It only counts toward the core catalog, and it can only be spent on the core catalog.** Limited runs and seasonal drops stay outside the mechanism entirely, both for earning and for redeeming. Scarcity has to stay real. A 150-unit run doesn't get diluted by a redemption, and nobody's path grows off a purchase that was never going to be reproducible.
- **The free pin has to ride along with a real order.** It can't be redeemed on its own — it gets added to a cart that already has something paid for in it. This isn't a workaround for cost; it's about what the moment feels like. A loose free pin arriving alone in an envelope is a consolation prize. Folded into an order someone was already excited about, it's a genuine surprise.

## How Charges Are Earned

The Collector's Path has to work no matter where someone actually bought the pin — our own site, a marketplace like Amazon or Etsy, our booth at a fair, or a retail partner's shelf. We don't control checkout on most of those, so the mechanism can't depend on us seeing the transaction happen.

**Every core-catalog pin ships with a private registration code, hidden inside the packaging until it's opened.** The customer registers it on our site to claim the charge on their own account. The code doesn't know or care which channel sold it — it only proves someone now holds a real pin. That single mechanism covers every channel identically:

- **Direct — online.** Our own site. A charge can register automatically for a logged-in account, no code-entry needed.
- **Direct — in person.** Our own booth at a fair or festival. Same as above; we control the point of sale, so we can credit the account on the spot.
- **Third-party marketplaces.** Amazon, Etsy, and similar. We have no visibility into who bought the pin and often no legal way to get their contact information — the code is what closes that gap.
- **Retail partners.** A regional gift shop, a themed retailer, a shelter. We have no access to their point of sale and don't need any — the code travels with the object, not the transaction.

A few things keep this honest:

- **Single-use, first-registration-wins.** Once claimed, a code is dead.
- **Hidden until opened.** Nobody can harvest codes off a shelf without buying the pin first.
- **Redemption is direct-only, regardless of where a charge was earned.** Charges can be earned anywhere a pin is legitimately sold. They can only ever be *spent* through us — never through a retailer, a marketplace, or anyone else. This is the one asymmetry in the whole system, and it's intentional: it's how we stay the ones doing the rewarding, no matter how the pin found its way to someone.

## Wholesale & Retail Partners

**Wholesale purchases don't earn charges, and they can't redeem them.** A retailer's purchase order is a business transaction, not a person building a collection. If a 50-unit order could fill someone's entire Collector's Path in one shot, the mechanism would stop meaning "we notice you've stuck with us" and start meaning "we notice you have a large budget." The Path lives entirely inside the relationship with the person who ends up wearing or displaying the pin — never the account of whoever restocked the shelf.

**Wholesale pricing isn't a violation of "we don't discount."** The philosophy protects against ever telling the person who loves the thing that what they paid wasn't really its worth. A retailer isn't that person — they're a partner who needs margin to justify carrying us at all. Standard wholesale terms are simply how that channel works, and they don't touch what any end customer ever pays.

**Regional and thematic placement is a second doorway into the same catalog, not a separate tier of it.** The core catalog — Hometown, Pup Club, and Zodiac — is defined by the series, not by where a given pin happens to sell. A Burlingame pin is core catalog whether it's bought on our site or off a shelf at a shop in Burlingame; a Pup Club pin earns and redeems the same whether it's bought online or at a shelter. Putting the right pin in the right place — dog pins where dog lovers already are, hometown pins where that hometown actually is — isn't a different business, it's the fastest way to put a pin in front of the person who was always going to love it, which is the entire premise of the Manifesto applied to distribution instead of design.

**What we offer retail partners instead of a discount:** the same principle as everywhere else in this document — reward the relationship, don't touch the price. Priority access to new designs before other accounts see them. Location-exclusive collaborations nobody else gets to carry. Better terms as a partnership proves out over time — payment terms that loosen, minimums that relax. None of it is a markdown on what they pay per unit; all of it rewards a retailer for being a real, ongoing partner rather than a one-time order.

## What follows from this

**Full price is the only price, always.** Every pin, every board, every strap. If someone pays full price on day one and full price a year later, that's not a failure of promotion — that's the point.

**Slow-moving inventory doesn't get marked down, it gets retired.** If a design isn't finding its people, the answer is to let it sell out and end, or bundle it with something that adds real value — never to cut the price until it moves. A discount bin is where love goes to become clearance.

**Rewards accrue to real customers, not anonymous traffic.** No public promo codes, no "first-time buyer" discounts that reward the least loyal person in the room more than the most loyal one.

**We can be generous — we just have to be generous the right way.** A surprise upgrade, a handwritten note, a bonus pin slipped into an order for someone who's bought five times — all fair game, all in keeping with who we are. Generosity aimed at someone we already know beats a discount aimed at no one in particular.

**The Collector's Path never touches a price tag, only a relationship.** Three, six, or nine charges filled doesn't make any single pin cheaper. It unlocks something added — access, an object, eventually a free pin — delivered alongside a real order. The number on any single pin, on any single day, never moves.

**A charge is earned the moment someone holds the pin, not the moment we happen to see the sale.** Online, in person, through a marketplace, off a retail shelf — if it's a real core-catalog pin, it counts. Where it's redeemed is the only place we draw a line: always with us, never through a partner.

---

## The test

Before we offer anything involving money — a discount, a promo, a reward, a code —one question:

*Does this make someone feel like they got a smart deal, or does it make someone feel like they belong here?*

If it's the first, it's not for us. If it's the second, ship it.
